# Story Architecture

The story is built on a deliberate skeleton. Never start writing scenes before the skeleton is fixed.

## The story skeleton

Fill each of these in order before drafting:

1. **Premise (प्रीमाइस)** — the one-line "what if" that makes the story worth telling.
2. **Protagonist (नायक/नायिका)** — who we follow, what they want, what they lack.
3. **Goal (लक्ष्य)** — the concrete, visible thing they are chasing.
4. **Conflict (संघर्ष)** — what stands in the way (person, force, or inner flaw).
5. **Stakes (दांव)** — what is lost if they fail; make it personal and clear.
6. **Inciting Incident (उत्प्रेरक घटना)** — the disruption that starts the journey.
7. **Rising Action (बढ़ता तनाव)** — escalating complications, each harder than the last.
8. **Mystery (रहस्य)** — the central question the audience wants answered.
9. **Clues (सुराग)** — planted fairly, each one payable off later.
10. **Midpoint Reversal (मध्य-बिंदु पलटाव)** — a turn that changes the goal or the stakes.
11. **Character Development (चरित्र विकास)** — the protagonist changes because of events.
12. **Major Setback (बड़ी असफलता)** — the low point where all seems lost.
13. **Climax (चरमोत्कर्ष)** — the final confrontation; the hero acts, not luck.
14. **Reveal (खुलासा)** — the mystery resolves using earlier planted clues.
15. **Resolution (समाधान)** — loose ends close; the new normal is shown.
16. **Moral (सीख)** — the lesson, shown through events, never lectured.

## The causal chain

Every scene must advance the story through this loop. No scene is "filler."

**Event → Reaction → Decision → Consequence → New Problem**

- **Event** — something happens.
- **Reaction** — characters respond emotionally/physically.
- **Decision** — a character chooses a course of action.
- **Consequence** — that choice produces a result.
- **New Problem** — the result raises the next question, restarting the loop.

If a scene does not create a *new problem* or answer an existing one, cut or rewrite it.

## Opening rules

- Open on a **compelling, specific situation** — a moment of tension, a strange discovery, an unusual choice.
- **Avoid generic openings** like "एक गाँव में..." / "बहुत समय पहले एक राजा था..." Start in motion, in a concrete place, at a charged moment.
- Establish the protagonist's want and the world's flavor within the first scene.
- Plant the first thread of the central mystery early.

## Pacing

- Target **12–18 meaningful scenes** for a ~20-minute story.
- **Every scene has a function** — advance plot, reveal character, plant/pay a clue, or raise stakes. Most do more than one.
- Vary scene length and intensity: fast scenes after slow ones, quiet beats after action.
- Escalate: each act should raise the stakes over the previous.

## Story DNA

Weave these seven strands **organically** — they emerge from character and situation, never bolted on:

- **Comedy (हास्य)** — light, character-driven humor.
- **Adventure (रोमांच)** — movement, discovery, a journey.
- **Mystery (रहस्य)** — the central question that pulls the audience forward.
- **Emotion (भावना)** — relationships and real feeling.
- **Excitement (उत्साह)** — momentum and high-energy beats.
- **Mild scary (हल्का डर)** — safe, family-friendly suspense; never traumatic.
- **Moral (सीख)** — an earned lesson embedded in the resolution.

Balance the strands so no single one dominates; family-friendly tone throughout.
