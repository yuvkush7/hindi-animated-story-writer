# Character Design

Characters carry the story. Design them deliberately; no filler characters.

## Supported cast sizes

Pick one of three cast sizes based on the request:

- **2 characters** — intimate, focused stories (a duo: partners, siblings, rivals).
- **5 characters** — a small ensemble with clear roles.
- **8 characters** — a fuller world with subplots.

**Default rule:** at least **one important female character** — not a background role, but someone who drives events.

## What every major character needs

For each major character, define:

- **Purpose (उद्देश्य)** — why they exist in the story; their story function.
- **Personality (व्यक्तित्व)** — a few sharp, specific traits (not "nice").
- **Goal (लक्ष्य)** — what they want in the story.
- **Relationship (रिश्ता)** — how they connect to the protagonist and others.
- **Distinct voice (विशिष्ट आवाज़)** — a recognizable way of speaking (word choice, rhythm, catchphrase).
- **Contribution (योगदान)** — the specific plot/theme work only they can do.

If a character has no unique contribution, **merge or cut them**. No filler.

## Compact character display format

Display each character in this one-line format:

```
नाम — उम्र — लिंग — छोटी पहचान / भूमिका
```

Examples:

```
तितली — 12 — स्त्री — जिज्ञासु और निडर लड़की, कहानी की नायिका
गोलू — 11 — पुरुष — तितली का शरारती दोस्त, हास्य लाता है
धनीराम — 55 — पुरुष — लालची साहूकार, मुख्य विरोधी
दादी माँ — 70 — स्त्री — गाँव की समझदार बुज़ुर्ग, पुराने राज़ जानती हैं
```

## Character development arcs

- Each major arc moves from a starting state to an earned changed state, e.g. **डरपोक → साहसी**, **लालची → उदार**, **अकेला → भरोसेमंद दोस्त**.
- The change must be **earned by events** in the story — a character grows because of what happens to them, not because the plot needs it.
- Show the arc across at least three beats: the flaw shown, the flaw tested, the flaw overcome.
- Keep voices distinct throughout so the audience can tell who is speaking even without the attribution tag.
