# Mystery Engine

The mystery is the engine that pulls the audience through the story. Track it deliberately.

## Track the information state

At every point in the story, know explicitly:

- **Known info (ज्ञात जानकारी)** — what the audience knows.
- **Unknown info (अज्ञात जानकारी)** — what is deliberately hidden.
- **Character knowledge** — what each character knows vs. the audience (dramatic irony).

Maintain a running ledger so you never accidentally reveal too early or forget to pay off.

## Clues (सुराग)

- Plant clues **fairly and in advance** of the reveal.
- **Every clue must pay off.** If a clue never matters, remove it or convert it into a red herring on purpose.
- Vary clue visibility: some obvious, some hidden in plain sight, some only meaningful in hindsight.

## Red herrings (झूठे सुराग)

- Use red herrings to misdirect — but they must be **fair**: they should have an innocent explanation that is revealed.
- Do not cheat the audience; a good red herring feels obvious *after* the reveal.

## False assumptions (गलत धारणाएँ)

- Let characters (and the audience) hold a reasonable but wrong belief.
- The reveal often works by overturning a false assumption everyone accepted.

## The reveal (खुलासा)

- The reveal must be **built entirely from earlier planted clues** — the audience could, in principle, have solved it.
- After the reveal, earlier scenes should read differently and make *more* sense.

## Forbidden resolutions

The mystery may **never** be solved by:

- **Random coincidence** — the answer just happening to appear.
- **Unexplained powers** — abilities never established earlier.
- **Sudden new characters** — someone appearing only to hand over the answer.
- **Arbitrary magic** — a magical fix with no rules or setup.

The protagonist must solve the mystery through **observation, reasoning, and the planted clues** — fair play always.
